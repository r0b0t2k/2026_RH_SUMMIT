# Open Source Enterprise AI and the Human in the Loop

- Date/Time (EDT): 2026-05-11, 1:00 PM - 2:00 PM
- Session Type: Community Day Session
- Source Archive Ref: 2026_11/enterpriseAI_OS_HumanInLoop

## Overview
This session argues that open source is becoming foundational to enterprise AI in the same way it previously became foundational to Linux and Kubernetes adoption. Speakers from Red Hat frame the current AI era as an ecosystem problem, not just a model-quality competition, and emphasize that open standards, transparent development, and multi-party governance will determine long-term enterprise outcomes.

The talk also highlights the operational realities of agentic AI: agents now read, write, and execute actions across enterprise systems, so governance, observability, and policy controls are required from day one. A panel discussion extends this into legal, contribution-policy, and community-maintainer concerns.

## Key Themes
- Open ecosystems compound faster than closed ecosystems.
- AI infrastructure must remain shapeable by users, not only rentable from providers.
- Agentic workflows increase security and governance requirements.
- Community sustainability and maintainer load are strategic risks.
- Enterprises need openness plus predictable support/compliance.

## Chronological Notes
### Why Open Source for AI
- Speakers draw continuity from GNU/Linux and Kubernetes to current AI infrastructure.
- Projects and standards named include PyTorch, vLLM, LLM-D, Kubeflow, KServe, and MCP.
- Core claim: meaningful AI progress is increasingly anchored in open foundations.

### Ecosystem vs. Single Model Framing
- The session asserts that model leaderboards alone are not the frontier.
- Value concentrates in harnesses, tooling, integration layers, and shared standards.
- Open collaboration is described as the mechanism for cumulative innovation.

### Risks, Governance, and Trust
- Discussion includes AI BOM and agent BOM needs for provenance and accountability.
- Speakers emphasize that openness without structure can create enterprise liability.
- Maintainer burnout and uneven contributor burden are called out as practical constraints.

### Agentic AI in Practice
- Agents are described as infrastructure actors, not just chat interfaces.
- Required controls include guardrails, observability, and policy boundaries.
- Audience examples include MCP servers, model switching, and eval-driven operations.

### Panel: Contribution and Policy
- Panelists discuss AI-assisted contribution norms across foundations and projects.
- Repeated guidance: contributors should disclose AI use and explain submitted code.
- Community-specific AI contribution policies are noted as non-uniform and evolving.

## Notable Takeaways
1. The strongest claim in this session is that open standards are becoming non-optional for durable AI strategy.
2. Agentic systems magnify supply-chain, policy, and observability requirements.
3. Enterprises are expected to demand both open innovation and production-grade controls.
4. AI contribution norms in open communities are still converging and project-specific.
